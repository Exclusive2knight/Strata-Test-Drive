# New world plan (not started; wait for the go-ahead)

Replaces Halden. Bigger than Halden. Built in stages, each playable when it lands.
Rough size: 20–30 hours of work.

## Kept from Halden
- The big car wash (CW site, props/carwash*.json)
- Traffic lights
- The parking garage (Fort St helix: GAR, garage_helix model)

Everything else that is a building gets replaced by the owner's own models
(stores, houses, gas stations, Tread Center). Until a model arrives, use a plain
placeholder box at the right spot and swap the model in later.

## Region layout
- **Big city:** downtown towers, freeway through the middle, the parking garage, most stores.
  A big river through it with several bridges.
- **Second city:** smaller and spread out, strip malls, the car wash, Tread Center, suburbs.
- **East Coast-style small town** (in the spirit of BeamNG's East Coast USA map, our own design):
  tree-lined two-lane roads, older wood-sided houses with porches and yards, a main street
  with a diner, a church and a gas station, rolling hills and woods, a lake and creeks.
- **Between them:** farmland, county roads, the freeway linking the cities.
- **The mountain** (replaces the off-road park): off-road trails climbing from the bottom
  (rock gardens, mud, steep switchbacks, hardest near the top, summit lookout),
  plus paved roads up the mountain (see below).
- **Proving ground:** rebuilt on flat land outside town, off the freeway,
  with all its test sections and the 40% hill.

## Water and bridges
- Big river through the big city: a large suspension/cable-stayed freeway bridge,
  city street bridges, maybe a drawbridge that lifts for boats.
- Freeway crossing a bay or wide river between the cities on a long, high bridge.
- Lake and creeks around the small town: old stone and covered wooden bridges.
- Mountain streams with small bridges; shallow fords on the trails.
- Deep water: the car stalls and sinks. Shallow fords splash but trucks/SUVs get through.
- **Janky bridges:** rickety wooden plank bridges that bounce and tilt (a missing plank or two),
  one-lane bridges where you wait for oncoming cars, old rusty steel truss bridges with a weight
  limit that creak and sag under heavy trucks, a low-water crossing that floods in heavy rain,
  a sketchy log bridge on the mountain trail.

## Mountain roads
- Switchback road up the face: tight hairpins, guardrails on the drop side, steep grades.
- Scenic pass road over the top: flowing curves, rock cuts, a short tunnel, an overlook, summit lookout.
- Narrow back roads: no center line, trees at the edge, blind corners, janky bridges.
- Runaway truck ramps and "use low gear" signs on steep downhills; snow and ice near the top in winter.

## Highways (make them realistic: all of this)
- **Real 3D interchanges, not flat:** the freeway rides on overpasses or dips under cross streets;
  ramps actually climb and drop with real grades. A big multi-level stack where two freeways cross,
  with long flyover ramps. Diamonds at city streets, cloverleafs where they fit.
- Long acceleration lanes on on-ramps, exit lanes that peel off, painted gore areas,
  "lane ends, merge left" lane drops. Traffic zipper-merges and moves over for merging cars.
- Numbered routes (interstate shields), exit numbers, green overhead signs with destinations and
  distances ("Exit 14 · Downtown · 1 mile"), mile markers, "next exit" signs; navigation reads them out.
- Wide shoulders with rumble strips, concrete median barriers or grass medians with cable barriers,
  lane counts that change (6 in the city, 4 in the country), speed limits by area,
  ramp meters at rush hour, a rest area with a truck stop.
- Highway traffic: more semi trucks keeping right, faster cars on the left, backups near busy
  interchanges, occasional crashes or construction closing a lane.
